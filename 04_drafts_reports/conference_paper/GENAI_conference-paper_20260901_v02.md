# Conference paper -- draft v02

**Benefit and concern are not opposites: independent dimensions in perceptions of
generative AI in higher education**

Target venue: ASCILITE 2026 · Abstract deadline 30 September 2026 · Full paper 15 November
Status: **draft, not for circulation outside the team**

## Abstract (draft, 187 words)

Public debate frames generative AI in higher education as a choice between benefit
and harm. We test whether users themselves hold that structure. A survey of 60
students and academic staff at one Australian university measured frequency of use,
perceived benefit, three categories of concern, disclosure behaviour and an overall
"benefit or problem" position, and 18 semi-structured interviews explored the
results. Perceived benefit and concern were statistically independent (Spearman
rho = 0.02, p = 0.88); concern did not predict the overall position (rho = -0.02),
while benefit did (rho = 0.50, p < 0.001). Interviews explain the independence:
participants realise benefit at the point of verification rather than at the point of
output, so a user with an effective verification practice can hold high benefit and
high concern without contradiction. We argue that the boon/bane framing measures
benefit while appearing to measure a net position, and that verification capability,
not attitude, is the variable that should be measured. We further find that the costs
users name -- capability erosion, privacy, and inability to judge others' use --
differ from the integrity-centred costs institutions debate.
