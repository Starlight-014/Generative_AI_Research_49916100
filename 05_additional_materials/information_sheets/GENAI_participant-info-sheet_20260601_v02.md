# Participant information sheet (version 02)

**Study:** Using Generative AI Tools: Boon or Bane?
**Approval:** HREC 2026/GENAI/0417 · **Version 02, 1 June 2026**

## What is this study about?

We are asking students and academic staff how they use generative AI tools in their
university work, what they find useful about them, and what concerns them. The aim is
to describe what people actually do, not to check whether anyone has broken a rule.

## What would I be asked to do?

A survey of about 6 minutes. If you wish, you can also volunteer for an interview of
about 40 minutes, in person or online. You can do the survey without volunteering for
an interview.

## Will what I say affect my marks or my employment?

**No.** The research team has no role in assessing you. Your responses are not linked
to your name in anything we analyse, and nothing you tell us is reported to your
course coordinator, your supervisor or your head of school. We are not asking you to
disclose misconduct and we do not want you to.

## What happens to my data?

- Survey responses are collected without your name and cannot be linked back to you.
- Interviews are audio recorded with your permission. The recording is stored on the
  University's secure research storage, transcribed, and then de-identified: your
  name and anything else that could identify you is removed before anyone analyses it.
- Only de-identified material is shared within the research team or published.
- Recordings are destroyed once the transcript has been checked and the study is
  published. De-identified data is kept for at least five years.

## Can I change my mind?

Yes. You can skip any question, stop an interview at any time without giving a
reason, and withdraw your data up to the point at which analysis begins. After that
your contribution cannot be separated from the pooled results.

## Will I be quoted?

Possibly, but only in de-identified form and only where the quote cannot identify
you. If something you say is specific enough that a colleague might recognise you, we
will come back to you and ask before using it.

## Who can I contact?

The chief investigator (contact details in the distributed version of this sheet), or,
if you have a concern about how the study has been conducted, the University's Human
Research Ethics office, independently of the research team.
